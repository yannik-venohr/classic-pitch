Examples

    Piano & Organ

  • Chopin – Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2 (2003)
    In this piano example, we observe:
    • Piano transcription works almost perfectly (strong onsets and perfect intonation).
    • Trills (0:36-0:38) are challenging and not transcribed at all.
    • On the other hand, turns (0:08-0:09, 2:36-2:40) are nicely captured.
    • The cadence-like passage (3:09-3:18) is challenging.
  • Chopin – Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2 (1927)
    In this piano example, recorded in 1927, we observe:
    • It has a less refined sound compared to the modern recording and some details might be not captured that well.
    • Onsets in the left hand are missed (2:16).
    • Turns are both transcribed (0:09) and missed (3:31-3:34).
    • Trills are still challenging, but in comparison to the modern recording, more onsets on the indicated note are detected (0:46).
    • Slower trill-like passages are transcribed more accurately (3:50).
    • Cadence-like passage is also challenging (4:12-4:24).
  • J. S. Bach – Fantasia in G minor BWV 542
    In this Organ example, we observe:
    • Mordent on first note missing (0:00).
    • Bb onset missing in 0:01.
    • At 0:24 the long pedal notes are nicely visible in the piano roll.
    • Offsets are not always accurate because of the sustained nature of the organ sound.
    • But overall, finding onsets in that full sound works reasonably well (e.g., 0:24).

    • Orchestral & Chamber Music

  • Mozart – Oboe Quartet in F major
    In this chamber music example, we observe:
    • Intonation is generally good, but there are some slight pitch variations.
    • Appoggiaturas are missed (0:14,violin) or not fully transcribed (1:37)
    • Breathing at the beginning is not transcribed, which is desired.
    • In this particular scenario, repeated notes on the same pitch are often well transcribed (0:22). Also for strings (0:28, 4:20).
    • Fast woodwind trills are detected (1:13).
    • Interruptive clapping is not transcribed (4:00)
  • Beethoven – Symphony No. 5

    Note: Audio begins after 0:08

    In this example, we observe:
    • Even Orchestra can be transcribed reasonably, but is hard to evaluate.
    • A passage that is easier to evaluate and well transcribed starts at 5:55-6:15. We observe many accurate onsets, also on repeated notes, and clean offsets.
    • For strings onsets are often not detected, especially when they are short and repeated on the same note (0:11).
    • Tremolos in the strings are difficult (0:38-0:41).
    • Timpani rolls are captured as onsets but across all pitches (3:58).

    • Vocal Music

  • Vocal Journey – Goodbye Love

    Note: Audio begins after 0:11

    In this choir example, we observe:
    • Sometimes multiple onsets are detected for the same note (0:14-0:18).
    • It can be hard to define offsets, especially in reverberant environments (1:30 vs. 1:53).
    • With clear vocals, the transcription is generally more accurate (e.g. 1:21-1:35).
    • Applause is correctly identified as unpitched sound (2:08).
    • Glissando can lead to challenges in pitch detection (e.g. two notes detected simultaneously).
  • Bellini – Casta Diva (from Norma)
    This example is from a live performance in 1958. We observe:
    • Tuning is not perfect, but the transcription is still reasonable.
    • Artifacts, such as coughing (0:12-0:29) or applause are ignored, while a cry (6:38) is captured as pitches.
    • Onset accuracy is not good, especially for strings. Often there are false positives (0:40, 1:28, 2:06)
    • The vocalization techniques of the soprano are sometimes captured as multiple onsets (2:00, 2:44). Especially stronger vibrato can cause problems.
    • The recording quality is not ideal, but the transcription overall is still usable.

    • Jazz & Pop/Electronic

  • John Coltrane – Giant Steps
    In this example, we observe:
    • Transcription is generally good, but there are some challenges with fast passages or complex playing techniques.
    • Non pitched instruments (drums) are not transcribed.
    • Fast tempo does not necessarily lead to poor transcription, but can be hard to evaluate.
    • Some saxophone parts are not transcribed (0:35).
    • Saxophone playing techniques such as vibrato or glissando are not transcribed (0:55-1:04).
    • Some glissandi are captured with two onsets (1:17,1:20).
  • Stevie Wonder – Sir Duke
    In this example, we observe:
    • Works well for most of the song. Drums are correctly not transcribed.
    • G# onsets missing at 0:06.
    • Sometimes we find additional onsets for the same note (0:13, 1:24).
    • At 2:30 the brass section is prominent.
    • Glissando identified as two notes (0:10).
    • Missing turn at 0:16.
    • Bass together with kickdrum is missed (0:21).
  • Fatima Yamaha – What's a Girl to Do
    In this example, we observe:
    • Works well for most of the song.
    • Non pitched instruments (drums) are not transcribed, but it is sometimes hard to distinguish them from pitched instruments. In 3:15-3:20 we hear a plucking-like sound and it is not transcribed, while in 3:30-3:35 it is partly transcribed as a pitched instrument.
    • Echoes are not transcribed (3:06-3:12).

  • Missing Fundamental
    Example starts at 3:25 and ends at 4:05. In this example, we observe:
    • Again, voice is pitched.
    • At 3:25, we hear different harmonics built on top of 110 Hz (A2). Our model identifies A2 the whole time.
    • At 3:53, we hear the 110 Hz fundamental frequency along with its harmonics. At 3:55, the fundamental frequency at 110Hz is removed and only the harmonics are left. Our model still predicts 110 Hz.
    • This shows something our human ear does naturally: it can reconstruct a fundamental frequency even when it's not actually there, just by recognizing the pattern of harmonics above it. Our model does the same thing.
