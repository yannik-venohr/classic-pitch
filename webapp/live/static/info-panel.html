ClassicPitch, live

Press Start listening and play something. The roll scrolls right to left with now at the red line; nothing is recorded, and what scrolls off the left edge is gone.

Why the right-hand strip looks different

The model runs on the last 6 seconds of audio, over and over — a new pass every quarter second or so. Every moment you hear is therefore predicted a couple of dozen times, each time with more of what came after it, and those passes are averaged. The dashed line marks where that averaging has had time to work:

  • Left of it — the average, decoded into notes with the same decoder the file apps use, onsets in yellow. This is the same picture the file apps draw.
  • Right of it — the newest pass alone, raw, with brightness straight from the probability. The model thinking out loud.

Watching a note cross that line is the interesting part: what arrives as a smear of candidate pitches usually resolves into one as soon as the model can hear where the note went.

Staff notation

The collapsed Staff notation panel engraves the same notes the roll decodes, on the same time axis — each notehead sits directly above its note's yellow onset, on the same grand staff the other two apps draw. Only the settled zone is notated, so notes appear at the dashed line rather than at now.

The key decides how each pitch is spelled and what the signature says. On Auto it is detected from what you have been playing and keeps up with a modulation; pick a key yourself and it stays put. A note is given an accidental only where it disagrees with the signature.

Thresholds

The two sliders are the same decoding parameters the other apps expose under "Inside the model", except here they cost nothing to move: the browser holds the raw posteriograms and decodes them while it paints, with a port of the file apps' decoder, so there is no round trip. A note starts at an onset peak at or above the onset threshold and lasts while the frame activation stays at or above the frame threshold; dips of up to 5 frames are bridged, and notes of 4 frames or fewer are dropped. Activation that did not become a note is still drawn — dimly — rather than thrown away.

If nothing shows up

  • Check the Level meter: no movement means the wrong input is selected, or the browser is holding the microphone permission.
  • The reading next to the sliders says how fast windows are coming back. Much below one a second and the display will lag noticeably behind the room.
  • Tuning is pinned to A=440 here, unlike the file apps, which estimate it per recording. An instrument far off concert pitch will transcribe worse live than it does from a file.
